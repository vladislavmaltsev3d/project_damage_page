More Details

What’s included

Project Damage 1.0 includes 64 effects and generators. The asset browser offers 71 choices when the eight Boolean Break variants are counted separately. The 1,512 presets in the extended library are starting settings for these tools.

Grease Pencil

What a stroke controls depends on the effect.

  • Place damage. Draw chips with Edge Damage, Corner Damage, Surface Damage or Damaged Edge Bevel. Place marks with Scratches Draw, Bullet Impacts, Metal Tear Draw, Rusty Holes or Clay.
  • Expose a structure. Draw openings with Concrete Damage and V2, Wood Damage, Lath Damage and its V2, Riven Lath, Stud Framing, Pipe Damage and Cable Damage. The standalone Rebars, Pipes, Wooden Laths, Studs and Bricks generators also accept damage drawings.
  • Cut and peel. Glass Drawn uses contours for holes. Wallpaper, Peeling, Paper Generator and Fabric Tears have drawing controls for their respective surfaces. Fracture, Cell Fracture, Ground Cracks and Ground Fissures use strokes to guide breaks.
  • Trace or scatter. Guide Ivy and Roots growth, trace Expanding Foam or Welded Edges, and mark regions for Grass Scatter and Gravel Scatter.

Fill & rigid-body physics

Fill Damage / Fill V2 reconstruct the missing volume for Edge, Corner and Surface Damage, Damaged Edge Bevel, Concrete Damage and V2, Hanging Rebar Holes, Pipe and Cable Damage, Lath Damage and V2, Riven Lath, Stud Framing and all eight Boolean Break variants. Bullet Impacts supports the original Fill Damage tool.

Use Fracture Fills to turn restored volumes into pieces. Cell Fracture can split the source mesh directly, including with its Glass pattern. Assign colliders, choose when pieces activate, then run or bake the simulation. Chain Reaction lets activation spread between pieces.

What the mesh keeps

Preserve Source Data carries UV maps, source materials, vertex groups and supported mesh attributes onto the result. Surviving faces keep their material assignments. UVs and weights are mapped onto rebuilt geometry. New fracture interiors can use their own material and inside-face UV map.

Damage zones get their own PD_… vertex groups by effect. Select a group to work on the affected vertices, or use its face mask to select the damaged faces along a shared rim. The editable source stays available while you build the stack.

Limits on complex meshes

Fill Damage reconstructs the difference between the source and the damaged mesh. Open boundaries, self-intersections or overlapping shells can produce an incomplete or unexpected fill. Inspect the restored volume before fracturing it.

In Cell Fracture, Guidance concentrates smaller pieces near a stroke. Knife Through projects a cut through depth and needs a stroke that divides a piece or joins an existing cut. Complex geometry can give uneven results or leave pieces uncut. Extend the stroke across the piece, or use a Cut Plane for a more predictable through-cut.

Before you
purchase.

A few things to know about the geometry, performance and animation workflow, so you can plan how Project Damage fits your projects.

Supported operating systems
Windows (64-bit) and macOS (Apple silicon)

Each platform has a separate add-on ZIP.

01 / TOPOLOGY

A retopology pass for game assets.

Procedural damage can create dense, triangulated areas and irregular edge flow. Clean, production-ready topology is not generated automatically. For game-engine assets, plan for retopology and optimization by hand or with your preferred external tools.

02 / PERFORMANCE

Detailed geometry takes calculation time.

A capable computer is recommended, especially for dense meshes and detailed damage. Even with the compiled C++ core, complex geometry needs time to rebuild after changes.

Manual Update lets you choose when to recalculate. Background Calculation moves supported calculations into a separate process, helping keep Blender responsive, while Draft Mode offers a lighter preview.

03 / SOURCE OBJECTS

Work from a static mesh.

Animated source objects, including rigged or deforming meshes, are not supported. Generate damage on a static source. This is separate from the supported rigid-body workflow, which can animate the generated fragments.

04 / PARAMETER ANIMATION

Damage settings are not animatable.

Keyframed animation of damage parameters is not supported. Settings are sent to an external compiled C++ core to rebuild the mesh; this workflow does not evaluate changing damage parameters on every animation frame.

I've put a lot of work into making Project Damage comfortable to use, even within these performance limits. Manual updates, background calculation and draft previews help you stay in control while building detailed results.

Going experimental.

Standard builds use one thread. The experimental Windows build uses multiple threads and can run up to 2× faster on some tasks.

You may encounter unexpected behavior. I’d love to hear your feedback.

A few
useful details.

What kind of geometry does it create?

Project Damage generates geometry for wear, fractures, cuts and structural details. Individual effects have their own mesh requirements: some need a solid volume, while sheet effects work on thin surfaces. Check the matching guide before building the final mesh.

Can I keep editing the original?

Yes. The generated result stays linked to an editable source while you work. You can switch between the original and the damage result. Apply All bakes the final stack and keeps a hidden editable source.

Is every effect a physics simulation?

Effects are primarily artist-directed geometry tools. Supported physics workflows can fill the damaged region, fracture it and simulate the resulting pieces. Timber and metal presets describe a visual treatment; they do not model engineering material strength.

How do I install it?

Use the add-on ZIP that matches your Blender version and platform. In Blender, go to Edit → Preferences → Add-ons → Install from Disk. Enable project_damage, then open its tab in the 3D Viewport sidebar. The previews on this page come from the Blender 5.2 build.

What about materials and documentation?

The add-on has a local user manual with installation instructions and individual effect guides. An optional Project Damage CC0 Materials pack connects separately through the add-on's Preferences. Damage materials and structural materials can be adjusted in the relevant effect settings.

Will the result match the previews?

Your source mesh, real-world scale, thickness and quality settings influence the result. Start with moderate detail, use Draft while placing damage and inspect the full-quality mesh before applying it.

We’d love your feedback 

My colleague and I built Project Damage together. The add-on and its generators span more than 200,000 lines of code. Before release, we spent a lot of time testing the tools, fixing issues, and refining the workflow.

With a tool this complex, some bugs only surface in real projects. If you run into an issue or have an idea for making the add-on better, we’d be grateful to hear from you. Your feedback helps us decide what to fix and improve next.
